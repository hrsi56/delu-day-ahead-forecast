## 1. Purpose and Scope

This is a portfolio-grade probabilistic forecasting project built for an industry Data Scientist hiring audience. The tool produces, on demand, an hourly forecast of the **German–Luxembourg (DE-LU) bidding-zone day-ahead electricity price** for the **next delivery day (normally 24 hourly values; 23/25 on the two DST-transition days, D+1)**, with calibrated 50% / 80% / 95% prediction intervals.

The target is the next delivery day specifically because that is what the day-ahead market clears: the EPEX SPOT day-ahead auction (within the EU Single Day-Ahead Coupling, cleared by EUPHEMIA) closes its order book at **12:00 CET on D-1, with preliminary results ~12:45 CET and final results ~12:57 CET**. At that gate the prices for all hours of D+1 are forecast, and every lagged-price feature is legitimately known. A multi-day-ahead target would force the model to consume values that do not yet exist at forecast time — look-ahead leakage in the one project whose centerpiece is a leakage audit. The 24h horizon keeps the feature catalog clean and the methodology airtight. (See Section 5.2.)

*Why the German market, for an Israeli interviewer:* the domain is a methodology vehicle, not a domain commitment — a real, noisy, public time-series with a textbook regime shift (the 2021–2023 European energy crisis), the world's most-studied negative-price phenomenon, and a published benchmark literature (EPEX-DE is one of the five canonical Lago et al. 2021 open-access markets), chosen so the work is verifiable rather than a toy. The methodology transfers to any consequential time-series. The full interview answer is prepared in interview prep, not here.

The constraints, named up front:

- **Interactive marimo showcase, statically deployed.** A reactive marimo notebook (server mode, not WASM) with a fixed quantile-level selector and at most one simple scenario control (§9.2), containerized via a multi-stage Dockerfile and deployed to Hugging Face Spaces free tier. **v6.7:** the champion is bundled in the image, updates are served by direct local inference, and the data is **one frozen snapshot** — there is no weekly refresh obligation and no live API pull during user sessions. **(v6.2)** The recruiter-facing **primary URL** is a statically exported HTML report of the full reading order on **GitHub Pages** — no container, no sleep state; the interactive Space is the deep-dive linked from it with an explicit wake label (Section 9.2).
- **Cloud-backed experiment tracking via DagsHub-hosted MLflow.** Public read-only MLflow UI linked from CV and LinkedIn. No DVC. Data reproducibility achieved via a single committed Parquet snapshot (CC BY 4.0 with attribution) plus pinned dependencies plus tagged commit.
- **Single model.** A monolithic LightGBM quantile ensemble (nine native quantile heads). No model menagerie, no neural challenger, no blending, no shadow logging.
- **CQR-calibrated quantile intervals.** Conformalized Quantile Regression (Romano et al. 2019) on the four symmetric quantile pairs, with isotonic monotonicity enforced last. Per-interval finite-sample marginal coverage **attaches to the post-CQR/pre-isotonic output; the final post-isotonic intervals are verified empirically** (**v6.3** three-stage coverage — §6.2, §8.4). No sequential conformal apparatus (EnbPI / SPCI) and no Giacomini-White gating.
- **Forecast + diagnostic report, not a trading product.** No LONG/SHORT/FLAT layer, no battery-arbitrage optimization, no bid-reference logic, no utility/risk-aversion parameter, no auto-generated natural-language commentary.

The audience is a Data Science hiring manager. The artifact must demonstrate: domain understanding, defensible feature engineering, correct time-series validation, sensible model choice, explainability, cloud-backed experiment tracking via a publicly inspectable MLflow UI, honest uncertainty communication, and a polished interactive interface deployed to a recognizable ML hosting target. Total compute footprint: trains end-to-end in minutes on an Apple Silicon M3, no GPU required. Total hosting cost: $0 (Hugging Face Spaces free tier + DagsHub free tier).

## 2. The DE-LU Day-Ahead Market: Context and Regime Shifts

DE-LU is the German–Luxembourg bidding zone (split from DE-AT-LU on **2018-10-01**; the project's 2019-01-01 window start sits cleanly inside the continuous DE-LU series). The day-ahead price clears in the EPEX SPOT auction within the EU Single Day-Ahead Coupling (SDAC), solved by the EUPHEMIA algorithm: order book closes **12:00 CET D-1**, results publish ~12:45–12:57 CET. Harmonized clearing-price bounds are **+4,000 / −500 EUR/MWh**; the cap auto-increases by +1,000 EUR/MWh after near-cap events (it rose +3,000 → +4,000 effective 2022-05-10, triggered by French hours of €2,712.99 and €2,987.78 on 2022-04-03; a further step to +5,000 was announced and then frozen). **The floor moves to −600 EUR/MWh from 2026-05-28** — a data note for the snapshot era, and a model-staleness note: the release is a **frozen snapshot, not a refreshed service** (§9.2), so whether that change falls inside the training window is fixed once by the published §7.1 snapshot cutoff and is never tracked by a recurring refresh.

The model must absorb a **three-regime non-stationarity**. These are not historical curiosities — they are the live structure of current pricing behavior.

### 2.1 The 2021–2023 energy crisis and its normalization

German day-ahead prices averaged ~€30/MWh across 2015–2020, then ~€97 in 2021, **~€235 in 2022** — with the **August 2022 monthly average near €469/MWh** as gas-fired plants set the marginal price in the vast majority of hours (day-ahead price vs. gas price correlation ~93% through the crisis; TTF peaked at €339/MWh on 2022-08-26). Normalization followed: €95.18 (2023), €78.51 (2024), €89.32 (2025, Bundesnetzagentur). The crisis is an exogenous structural break of textbook clarity — it ran for ~18 months and is cleaner than any single-storm event.

The model carries two explicit regime features encoding this timeline: `crisis_period` (binary, 2021-09-01 ≤ t ≤ 2022-12-31 — the gas run-up through the peak year; the invasion date 2022-02-24 is the hard exogenous anchor inside it) and `post_crisis` (binary, t ≥ 2023-01-01). Together they encode the pre-crisis / crisis / post-crisis partition. Both are first-class features (Section 4), and the Month-1 change-point block validates the boundaries empirically rather than assuming them — the detector should land near the autumn-2021 run-up and/or the early-2022 shock, and near the late-2022 normalization.

### 2.2 Solar build-out and the negative-price era

The post-crisis regime is not a return to 2019. German PV capacity reached ~100 GW by early 2026 (wind ~73 GW); the last three nuclear plants closed **2023-04-15** (treated as a secondary covariate, not a hard regime boundary — the crisis dominates the price record). The structural consequence is **solar cannibalization and a negative-price explosion**: official Bundesnetzagentur counts of negative day-ahead hours run 139 (2021) → 69 (2022, crisis-suppressed) → 301 (2023) → 457 (2024) → **573 (2025, a record)**, with a single hour at the **−500 floor in 2023** and a 36-consecutive-hour negative run the same year. The mechanism — high renewable infeed meeting must-run and subsidized inflexible generation — is reinforced by market-premium rules that historically paid producers through negative hours (the rule has tightened stepwise; the Solarspitzengesetz era continues that direction). Scarcity has its own modern face: **Dunkelflaute** (low wind + low solar + high load), e.g., the December 2024 / January 2025 events that pushed day-ahead hours toward €583/MWh and triggered a Bundeskartellamt probe (which found no abuse).

**Implication for the model.** The price distribution is non-stationary in three regimes with two distinct stress directions — high-price scarcity (crisis, Dunkelflaute) and negative-price abundance (solar middays). The system therefore mandates walk-forward CV spanning all three regimes with both stress directions represented in eval folds, explicit regime indicator features, and per-regime calibration checks — all covered below. The 2019→pull-date training window (Section 3) spans the full arc while keeping the pre-crisis era from dominating the data the forecast actually serves.

## 3. Data Sources

## 4. Feature Catalog

Approximately 25 features, grouped by source. Every feature is classified as either **known-at-forecast-time (KFT)** — publicly available before the 12:00 CET D-1 gate — or **lagged-only (LAG)**. This distinction is the foundation of the leakage audit in Section 5.2. **v6.3:** the DA VRE forecast (A69) and everything derived from it are **neither KFT nor LAG-eligible for the champion** — A69 is published *after* the gate (§3, §5.2), so it is **excluded from the champion schema** and appears only in the §7.2 post-gate benchmark; the champion substitutes gate-feasible proxies **where the M2/CP-2 ablation retains them** (§4.1), else ships on the strict-gate base. Because the target is the next delivery day (D+1, Section 1), every LAG feature below is legitimately available at forecast time: the most recent cleared DA price is for day `D0`, and a 24h lag of a `D0+1` target lands on `D0`.

### 4.0 Time indexing, DST, and the 15-minute transition (mandatory)

The entire pipeline is indexed on **UTC**. `entsoe-py` returns tz-aware `Europe/Berlin` timestamps; convert to UTC on ingest and compute all lags in UTC so 24h = 24 rows, 168h = 168 rows, always. The spring-forward day carries **23 local hours** and the fall-back day **25** (a repeated local hour) — hours are never dropped or duplicated ad hoc; the user-facing D+1 vector therefore contains 23, 24 or 25 values as the delivery day requires, published as UTC-derived local hours with an explicit DST note. **15-minute MTU boundary (2025-10-01):** post-transition quarter-hours are averaged to hourly per §0 item 4; the hourly series must be continuous across the boundary with the post-transition hourly value equal to the mean of its four quarter-hours (**spike-validated on samples to 1e-9**).

**Spike-established (2026-06-12):** the load and VRE feeds are **natively PT15M across the whole 2019→present archive** — the quarter-hour→hour mean aggregation applies to the feature feeds **uniformly from day one**; only the price needs date-conditional handling. **Chunk-boundary bin integrity (mandatory):** the yearly chunked pulls produce partial hourly bins where a pull window starts or ends mid-hour on a PT15M series; ingestion must complete bins across chunk edges or drop-and-recover them, and an assertion guards that every aggregated hourly value is built from exactly four quarter-hours (extended invariant test 4, Section 9.4). Left unhandled, this silently loses ~1 hour per chunk boundary. **A03 expansion is the invariant; the library is the default implementation (v6.7, observed live 2026-09-07).** The sampled DE-LU day-ahead price document carried **`curveType A03` — variable-sized blocks** — with non-contiguous `<position>` values (94/90/92 points against `max_pos=96` on three of four TimeSeries). Pinned `entsoe-py` 0.8.0 expands the repeated positions correctly to a gap-free 15-minute grid with zero nulls and is the recommended ingest path. An alternative parser is permitted only if a committed test proves the same A03 expansion before full-bin validation; merely counting XML points would fail closed **on valid data**. All calendar and aggregation invariants are guarded by tests (Section 9.4) — they correct the integer-lag corruption that otherwise silently hits ~2 shoulder-season days per year plus every PT15M row.

Same-hour-tomorrow value and rolling 24h forecast level.

**VRE forecasts (post-gate — NOT KFT; benchmark-only, v6.3)** — DA wind-onshore, wind-offshore, and solar generation forecasts (A69/A01). ### 4.1 Champion catalog: two frozen candidates and one comparison (v6.7)

**v6.7 framing.** The champion catalog and the rule that selects it are **frozen here, before fitting**. There are exactly two candidates and one comparison. The v6.3 four-catalog experiment, its fitted A75 climatology, the `expected_scarcity_proxy` arm, the exact-Decimal adjudication contract, the canonical CSV/JSON byte contracts, the committed identity manifests, the label-blind review and the deterministic tie-break are all **retired** — the apparatus was disproportionate to the decision it protected.

**Candidate 1 — `base` (strict gate).** Calendar and static regime features (§4.0); the A65/A01 day-ahead load forecast; lagged day-ahead prices at 24h/48h/168h; and closed-left rolling price statistics.

**Candidate 2 — `base + residual_load_proxy`.** The base plus exactly one gate-feasible merit-order feature, defined as a plain lag feature:

> `vre_norm(d,h)` = the mean of aggregate A75 **actual** generation (wind-onshore + wind-offshore + solar) at Europe/Berlin local clock hour `h`, over the trailing **42 complete delivery days ending at the close of D-2**.
>
> `residual_load_proxy(d,h) = load_fc(d,h) − vre_norm(d,h)`

**Why a trailing window rather than a fitted climatology.** Three properties, all checkable:

1. **It is a LAG feature by construction**, so the machinery v6.3 needed does not apply. It consumes only actuals published through D-2 — Regulation 543/2013 Art. 16(1)(b) is aggregated generation by production type and Art. 16(2)(b) requires it no later than one hour after the operational period, so a D-2 window is available with a wide margin before the 12:00 D-1 gate. There is no proper-training partition rule, no per-fold refit, no fit-lineage invariant, and no fit-poisoning oracle, **because there is no fit**. It is audited exactly like `price_lag_168`.
2. **It can track the build-out; a fixed seasonal-diurnal basis has no mechanism to.** German installed solar capacity rose from 48.9 GW at end-2019 to 117 GW at end-2025. The v6.3 basis was re-estimated per fold but carried no capacity or trend term, so it risked bias toward older capacity levels in exactly the recent folds where the model is judged. A trailing window adapts without fitting anything. Whether it improves *price* forecasts is an empirical question for the comparison below — it is not asserted in advance.
3. **It is ~20 lines** against a 38-coefficient basis, its excluded-row accounting, its coefficient persistence, and its positive-control oracle.

**DST contract — target day and window alike.** `h` is the Europe/Berlin local clock hour while every row retains its canonical UTC identity; only the grouping key is local. *Target day:* on the fall-back day both repeated local-hour rows receive the same historical mean, and no row is synthesized for the absent spring-forward hour. *Inside the window:* every observation bearing that local clock hour participates in the mean, so a 42-day window contributes 43 observations at hour 02 across a fall-back boundary and 41 across a spring-forward boundary. Both sides are covered by a §9.4 test.

**Window length.** Forty-two days (six weeks) is pinned as a simple bias/variance compromise — enough observations to smooth daily weather noise while remaining responsive to recent season and capacity levels. It is a design choice fixed here, never selected or tuned against results.

**Runtime consequence, stated rather than discovered.** The champion now consumes **three feeds at inference** — lagged A44 prices, the A65/A01 load forecast, and 42 trailing days of A75 — where v6.3's frozen-coefficient climatology consumed two. The bundled showcase is snapshot-served and unaffected; the fresh-data CLI makes one additional query. This is an ordinary lag dependency of the same class as the price lags.

**A75 revision caveat.** A75 actuals are subject to later revision, so the trailing window uses the archive's current values rather than the values visible in real time. This is the same class of assumption as the A65 pre-gate assumption (§3, §5.2) and is disclosed the same way — once in §5.2 and once in the public limitations.

**The one comparison, and the rule that decides it.** Both catalogs run on the same five pinned development folds (§5.1) with matched rows, targets, seeds, hyperparameters, tuning budget and fitting/calibration boundaries. The decision metric is the **pooled observation-weighted raw-head mean pinball loss** across all nine quantiles: with `T={0.025,0.05,0.10,0.25,0.50,0.75,0.90,0.95,0.975}` and `ρ_τ(u)=max(τu,(τ−1)u)`, `L_c = Σ_f Σ_i Σ_τ ρ_τ(y_i−q̂_c,i,τ) / (9 Σ_f n_f)` — weighted across every row, fold and quantile, not an unweighted mean of five fold means. CQR and isotonic belong to M2's final fit and never enter this metric; raw quantile crossing is neither repaired nor rejected here.

**The augmented catalog ships only if its unrounded stored pooled loss is lower than `base`'s. Equality defaults to `base`.** Both losses, their percentage difference, and the selected catalog are reported. This is an ordinary development-stage model-selection decision, labelled as such — not a confirmatory result — and it needs no blinding, no adjudication verdict and no identity artifact. The Integration review verifies that the rows were matched and that the declared one-line rule was applied.

**"The domain feature did not earn its place, and here is the number" is a valid and publishable outcome.** The champion then ships as `base` with no forced headline. SHAP at M2 *explains* the retained features; it is not the incremental-value test.

**`expected_scarcity_proxy` is retired** and does not return as a third candidate. Its design reasoning — why `relu`, and why a product rather than a ratio, since a ratio cannot encode an AND condition — is preserved in §13 as an idea that was scoped out. `forecast_uncertainty_proxy` and `gas_to_load_ratio` remain out of scope.

## 5. Methodology: Walk-Forward CV and Data Leakage Audit

The two methodology choices below are the top sources of overstated results in the EPF literature (Lago et al. 2021) and the top questions a DS interviewer asks about a time-series project. Both are spelled out explicitly so they live in the final report.

### 5.1 Walk-forward CV, the five tail partitions, and the one-delivery-day embargo — pinned scheme (v6.7)

Validation uses **expanding-window walk-forward CV with five development folds**, each with a **one-complete-delivery-day embargo gap** and a **90-day eval block**, anchored so the eval blocks span **all three regimes and both stress directions** (≥2 pre-crisis, 1 crisis-peak, ≥2 post-crisis including a negative-price-stress block). A complete delivery day contributes 23, 24 or 25 hourly rows as DST requires; the embargo is not hard-coded as 24 rows. Concentrating the blocks in the recent tail would make the regime-stratified table (§8.3) un-fillable from eval folds and would violate the EPF principle of testing across regimes.

**Pinned development folds** (training window 2019-01-01 → the M1 pull date; each fold trains on all data up to its eval start minus the one-delivery-day embargo; expanding — each training set is a superset of the prior):

| Fold | Eval block | Regime |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2020-07-01 → 2020-09-28 | pre-crisis (COVID-recovery summer — flagged as atypical-demand in the stratified read; training span ~1.5y, the thinnest fold by design) |
| 2 | 2021-04-01 → 2021-06-29 | pre-crisis — the last clean window before the autumn-2021 gas run-up |
| 3 | 2022-07-01 → 2022-09-28 | **crisis peak** — contains the ~€469/MWh August-2022 month; the high-price stress block |
| 4 | 2025-05-01 → 2025-07-29 | post-crisis; **solar/negative-price stress block** — the record-negative-hours summer (573 negative hours in 2025, summer-clustered) |
| 5 | the latest 90-day block ending before Embargo A (dates pinned at the pull) | post-crisis; **fully post-15-min-transition**. It is the **latest development fold** — v6.6's "final 90 days ending at pull-date" definition is retired |

**The five tail partitions (v6.7).** At M1, after completeness filtering and **before any EDA or model development**, five non-overlapping partitions are pinned by complete delivery day:

| # | Partition | Length | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fold 5** | 90 days | latest development fold; joins Folds 1–4 for selection, diagnostics and DM reporting |
| 2 | **Embargo A** | 1 day | enters no fit, calibration or evaluation |
| 3 | **Final calibration** | 60 days | excluded from all development folds, selection, EDA and diagnostics; used **once**, after selection, to estimate the final CQR thresholds |
| 4 | **Embargo B** | 1 day | enters no fit, calibration or evaluation |
| 5 | **Holdout** | 90 days | ends at the snapshot cutoff; excluded from every fold, fit, calibration slice, EDA and development diagnostic; evaluated **exactly once** (§7.1) |

**The holdout inherits Fold 5's old role** as the current-tail headline evaluation: it is the most recent partition and the one measured on the quarter-hour-averaged hourly series the deployed product actually serves.

**No derived pull date is pinned.** The tail spans 242 delivery days from Fold 5's first day to the snapshot cutoff, but that constant goes stale the moment any window changes. CP-1 asserts the property directly instead: `min(fold_5.delivery_date) >= 2025-10-01`.

**The embargo is a principle, not a single boundary.** Its stated job is to break the autocorrelation adjacency between the last fitted target and the first measured feature. That adjacency exists at *every* fit/measure boundary, so one day separates each of them: final training → calibration (Embargo A), and calibration → holdout (Embargo B). **The same rule applies inside each development fold**, where §6.2's calibration slice would otherwise sit flush against proper-training. Total cost across the whole plan: seven delivery days.

**Why one delivery day suffices given 48h/168h lags (verbatim reasoning, market-relabeled).** The embargo's only job is to break the autocorrelation adjacency between the **last training target** and the **first validation feature**. A 168h-lagged feature of a validation row reaches back into the training period — but using a known, already-cleared historical price as an input feature is not leakage; it is exactly what the production model has at the 12:00 CET gate. One complete delivery day (one full target horizon, including its 23/25-hour DST form) removes the only true adjacency. Document this reasoning verbatim for the interviewer.

**Recalibration cadence.** At each development fold boundary, re-fit the LightGBM heads and CQR thresholds. `residual_load_proxy` requires no re-fitting of any kind — it is a trailing-window lag feature, not a fitted layer. Within a 90-day block, predict D+1 sequentially without peeking. Per-day recalibration is out of scope under the $0 compute ceiling; per-fold recalibration is the stated lean substitute (§0 item 1).

**Evidence classes, stated once.** The five development folds carry **development-stage, post-selection descriptive** evidence: they serve catalog selection, tuning and regime/stress diagnosis, and their DM p-values are labelled `development_post_selection`, never confirmatory superiority. The holdout carries one **pre-specified one-shot** result (§7.1). The v6.4 forward confirmatory audit — its future window, power contract, `selection_cutoff`, quarantine manifest, pre-registration and `PENDING_UNDERPOWERED` / `CONFIRMED` / `NOT_CONFIRMED` statuses — is **retired at v6.7**: waiting on future delivery days is a schedule trap with no defined end, and the frozen holdout answers the same question inside the snapshot that already exists.

### 5.2 Data leakage audit

Every feature in Section 4 is classified KFT or LAG with explicit justification. The D+1 target makes the LAG features legitimate: no LAG feature consumes a value dated on or after the delivery day.

- **KFT features.** Calendar and static-regime features are known by construction. **A65/A01 is pre-gate by explicit assumption** under Regulation 543/2013 Art. 6(1)(b)/(2)(b) (§3) — corroborated by the 2026-06-12 observation, never described as demonstrated. **The same article also requires the forecast to be "updated when significant changes occur," so the archived vector used for training may differ from the vector available at 12:00; that revision channel is part of this disclosed assumption, not a separate one.** The assumption is stated wherever the leakage audit is claimed: here, in the README limitations, and on the static page. An assumption stated plainly is defensible; an assumption presented as a measurement is not. `dunkelflaute_flag` is **not** in the champion's KFT set — it consumes the post-gate A69 forecast (see the VRE bullet).
- **The A75 trailing window (LAG, v6.7).** `vre_norm` consumes aggregate A75 actual generation only through the close of **D-2** — the Art. 16(1)(b) item, published under Art. 16(2)(b) within one hour of the operational period — so it clears the 12:00 D-1 gate with a wide margin. It is audited exactly like `price_lag_168`. **Second disclosed assumption:** A75 actuals are subject to later revision, so the window uses the archive's current values rather than the values visible in real time. This is the same class of assumption as the A65 one above and is disclosed in the same places. Because nothing is fitted, there is no proper-training partition rule and no fit-lineage to audit.
- **VRE forecasts — the strict-gate resolution (R-2), v6.3.** Observed 2026-06-12: the TP's A69/A01 for delivery day D is **not yet published at 15:35 CEST D-1** — initial publication lands in the 15:35–18:00 window, *after* the 12:00 gate. **This is resolved by construction, not by convention:** delivery-day A69 is **post-gate → not KFT → excluded from the champion runtime schema.** The champion carries only gate-feasible runtime features — the strict-gate base, plus `residual_load_proxy` (§4.1, built from the KFT load forecast and the D-2-bounded A75 window) where the two-arm comparison selects it. Delivery-day A69 and features computed from it enter **only** the explicitly-labeled **post-gate information benchmark** (§7.2), which quantifies what post-gate information would be worth but never ships. There is **no lagged-vintage implementation choice** inside the four catalogs: a lagged-A69 alternative is future work. The archive-overwrite harness (first snapshot 2026-06-12 for delivery 2026-06-13) remains benchmark provenance evidence. Revision metadata (`revisionNumber`, `createdDateTime`) is a confirmed dead end (constant / equal to pull wall-clock). The strict-gate posture is a stronger interview asset than as-archived honesty: the shipped model is feasible at 12:00 by construction.
- **LAG features** use `t−1` or earlier relative to the forecast origin `D0`. Rolling statistics (including the negative-hour count) use a strict `t−1` right edge, closed-left.
- **Three audited vectors:** (i) rolling-quantile and rolling-count features computed closed-left so the target is never in its own window; (ii) the VRE-forecast vintage (above); (iii) `crisis_period`/`post_crisis` leak nothing as constants but are collinear with time inside any single-regime fold — a further reason for the three-regime scheme (Section 5.1).
- **Champion/benchmark schema firewall (v6.7).** Two enforced **runtime** feature schemas. The **champion runtime schema forbids delivery-day A69, every feature computed from that post-gate vintage, and every same-day actual-value column** — while **permitting lagged actuals through D-2**, which is what admits `residual_load_proxy` and its `vre_norm` input as ordinary LAG features. The **post-gate benchmark runtime schema** explicitly permits delivery-day A69 and its named derivatives and nothing else new. The §9.4 invariant asserts exactly that separation. The v6.3 fit-lineage clause is retired with the fitted climatology it guarded: there is no fit, so there is no lineage to poison.
- **Target transform.** Do **not** apply log/Box-Cox to the target — the DE-LU DA price is *routinely* negative (573 hours in 2025) and has hit the −500 floor. Pinball loss, the CQR score, the additive conformal shift, and monotone rearrangement are all location-equivariant and behave correctly with negative targets and zero-crossing intervals (Section 6). If variance stabilization is ever wanted, use `asinh` (defined on ℝ); with LightGBM quantile heads no transform is needed. In this market the no-transform line is not an edge-case caveat — it is a demonstrated capability.

This audit is the single best preparation against the standard interview question *"How did you make sure your features weren't leaking?"*

## 6. Model: LightGBM Quantile Ensemble with CQR Calibration

*(The LightGBM/CQR/isotonic methodological core transfers from v5.5; v6.7 aligns the packaged artifact, feature preprocessing, benchmark boundary, and release path with the reduced contract.)*

### 6.1 Single monolithic LightGBM, packaged as one model

The target is the hourly DE-LU day-ahead price for the next delivery day. The model is a **single LightGBM regressor family** predicting the nine native quantiles `{p2.5, p5, p10, p25, p50, p75, p90, p95, p97.5}` — one head per level, each fit independently with `objective='quantile'`.

**The champion is one artifact, not nine.** The nine quantile boosters, the selected frozen catalog's feature pipeline, the **CQR thresholds** (Section 6.2), and the final isotonic step are wrapped in a single custom `mlflow.pyfunc.PythonModel` exposing one `predict` that returns the nine calibrated, monotone quantiles. There is **no fitted VRE climatology, coefficient persistence, proper-training standardization, or fit-lineage layer**: if the augmented catalog wins, its `residual_load_proxy` is computed from lagged A75 observations ending at D-2 (§4.1/§5.2). The runtime schema rejects delivery-day A69 and same-day actual-value columns while permitting that D-2-bounded LAG input (§9.4). The champion is logged as a run artifact and may also be registered, non-gating, as a model version carrying the current `champion` alias and release/lineage tags (§9.1); the released Space loads the **bundled artifact**, never a registry URI (§9.2). **The §7.2 post-gate benchmark is a raw-head evaluation only: no separate calibration, deployable artifact, registry version, or runtime path exists.**

### 6.2 CQR calibration, and the post-processing order

LightGBM's native quantile heads have no finite-sample coverage guarantee, and independently fit heads can cross. Both are handled in order:

1. **Raw LightGBM** produces nine quantile predictions per row (may cross).
2. **CQR (Romano, Patterson & Candès 2019)** on each symmetric pair. For each interval `(lo, hi) ∈ {(p2.5,p97.5),(p5,p95),(p10,p90),(p25,p75)}` at miscoverage `α ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.50}`, compute on the calibration set the conformity score `E_i = max{ q̂_lo(x_i) − y_i , y_i − q̂_hi(x_i) }`, take the threshold `Q_(lo,hi)` as the `⌈(n_cal+1)(1−α)⌉`-th smallest `E_i`, and form `[q̂_lo − Q, q̂_hi + Q]`. The median (p50) is left un-shifted for the point forecast. **Binding indexing rule (v6.4):** `k = ceil((n_cal+1)(1−α))` is a one-based rank; after ascending sort a zero-based implementation reads `scores[k−1]`, never `scores[k]`, and never substitutes an interpolated default quantile. Require `1 ≤ k ≤ n_cal`; an undersized calibration set raises a clear error instead of silently clipping. Under the formal conformal construction an out-of-range upper rank corresponds to the augmented `+∞` order statistic, so clipping would overstate the guarantee. The pinned production calibration slices are far above the finite-threshold minimum for `α=0.05`.
3. **Isotonic regression (last)** across the nine conformalized endpoints per row, as the final ordering guard.

**Calibration set (pinned; embargoed at v6.7):** the **final 60–90 days of each fold's training partition (~1,440–2,160 hourly rows)**, held out and disjoint from both proper-training and eval, refit per fold, with **a one-day embargo between proper-training and the calibration slice** so the same fit/measure rule applies here as at the §5.1 tail boundaries — the contract does not hold two rules for one hazard. The tradeoff is stated once: those rows leave proper-training — negligible against years of training data, and it keeps the conformity scores on the most-recent (most regime-relevant) data.

**Mandatory hand-checkable CQR fixture (v6.4).** The raw synthetic fixture has rows `j=0,…,19` with `(q̂_lo,j, q̂_hi,j, y_j)=(100,140,111−j)`. Therefore `E_j=max{100−(111−j),(111−j)−140}=j−11`, giving the ascending scores `−11, −10, …, 8` without supplying the answer as the only input. For `n_cal=20`, the four `α={0.05,0.10,0.20,0.50}` pairs must produce one-based ranks `{20,19,17,11}`, zero-based positions `{19,18,16,10}`, and thresholds `Q={8,7,5,−1}`. The fresh read-only **M2/CP-2 Integration Critic** recomputes the scores from the raw `(q̂_lo,q̂_hi,y)` rows and all four thresholds independently, verifies that negative `Q` narrows its pair, verifies that p50 is unchanged by CQR, and then recomputes the four real-fold thresholds from persisted calibration predictions. Isotonic remains a separate final operation.

**Why CQR, and why isotonic is still last (verbatim).** For a **single already-ordered interval pair**, a nonnegative CQR threshold widens the pair and cannot invert its two endpoints; a negative threshold narrows it and can. That statement is only pairwise: the raw heads may already cross, and the four pair-specific thresholds can leave the **nine** post-CQR endpoints globally unordered for either threshold sign. Isotonic therefore remains the unconditional final ordering guard. **Coverage claim (v6.3 — three stages, stated precisely):** the finite-sample, distribution-free **per-interval marginal guarantee ≥ 1−α under exchangeability attaches to the post-CQR / pre-isotonic output** (stage 2). Isotonic is a monotone rearrangement applied last (stage 3) and is inactive only when all nine post-CQR endpoints are already non-decreasing. Whenever it moves an endpoint it perturbs the interval, so **the coverage of the final post-isotonic output (stage 3) is reported as empirical**, verified on the reliability diagram (§8.4) at all three stages (raw / post-CQR / final). Do **not** claim the formal guarantee for the final output, and do **not** claim joint/simultaneous coverage across the four intervals; both caveats are stated.

Stated in the final report verbatim, for interview defense:

> *CQR provides finite-sample marginal coverage guarantees under exchangeability. The walk-forward CV mildly violates exchangeability — the crisis regime is not exchangeable with the pre-crisis regime, and the solar-driven negative-price era is not exchangeable with either — so empirical coverage may diverge from nominal on regime-shift folds. This is documented in the reliability diagram (Section 8.4).*

That paragraph is the heart of the project's uncertainty story.

### 6.3 Why a single zonal target, not components

The PJM version defended a monolithic target against an SMP/MCC/MLC decomposition. DE-LU needs no such defense: the day-ahead clearing price is a **single zonal price by construction** — there is no congestion/loss decomposition to model separately at the bidding-zone level. The architectural simplicity is inherited from the market design; the section is retained so the interview answer exists: *"DE-LU is one coupled zonal price; decomposition modeling is a nodal-market question, not a bidding-zone question."* (Intra-zonal redispatch costs are a grid-operations quantity, not a component of the DA clearing price, and are out of scope.)

## 7. Benchmarks and Statistical Testing

LightGBM is compared against:

- **Similar-day naïve (headline comparator)** — the exact Lago et al. (2021) EPF convention: for Tue–Fri, forecast = same hour of the previous day; for Mon/Sat/Sun, forecast = same hour of the same weekday one week prior. Matching the published convention keeps the DM results comparable to the benchmark literature.
- **Seasonal-naïve 168h** — forecast = price 168 hours ago. Week-over-week seasonality baseline.
- **Ridge regression** with the same engineered feature catalog. Classical baseline; checks whether the boosted-tree complexity earns its place (and reuses the Month-0 OLS/normal-equation work).

Metrics: **MAE** on the median forecast, **mean pinball loss** across the nine quantiles, **empirical coverage** at the 50% / 80% / 95% nominal levels.

### 7.1 Diebold-Mariano tests, and the one-shot holdout

*(Machinery carried from v5.5; comparator relabeled.)* Two DM tests are reported, both against the similar-day naïve:

**(a) Point accuracy — DM on median absolute error.** Loss differential on the daily median forecast.

**(b) Probabilistic skill — DM on the mean pinball-loss differential.** Per hour `h` and day `t`, per quantile `τ`, pinball loss `L_τ(t,h) = max(τ·(y−q̂_τ), (τ−1)·(y−q̂_τ))`; let `H_t` be the actual number of local delivery-hour rows on day `t` (23, 24 or 25), then day-level model loss is `L_model(t) = (1/H_t)Σ_h (1/9)Σ_τ L_τ(t,h)` and differential `d_t = L_model(t) − L_naive(t)`. One-sided DM `= mean(d)/sqrt(LRV(d)/N)`, **LRV via Newey–West** with lag truncation `⌊N^{1/3}⌋`; reject (model better) if DM < −1.645. Use the **multivariate, pooled-across-hours** daily-vector form as the headline; report per-hour DM only as a diagnostic. Averaging pinball across the nine levels is a discrete approximation to CRPS; explicit CRPS is optional. The UTC index makes the DST-day row count mechanical.

**Development evidence.** Because the five pinned folds are also used for catalog selection and model development, their DM p-values are explicitly labelled **descriptive post-selection evidence**, `evidence_class = development_post_selection`. They are **reported, never gated**: no p-value threshold controls M2 advancement (§12). The ≥15% pinball improvement remains a narrative target only.

**The one-shot holdout evaluation (v6.7).** After the catalog, hyperparameters and every implementation choice are frozen at CP-2, the nine raw heads are fit once on every eligible row **strictly before Embargo A**, fixed-model predictions are generated for the 60-day calibration slice, the four CQR thresholds are estimated, isotonic-last is attached, and that complete artifact is frozen. It is then evaluated **exactly once** on the 90-day holdout. Forecasts are generated sequentially, so earlier holdout prices and A75 actuals may enter later holdout rows **only** through the already-declared LAG rules; no holdout outcome may enter a fit, a threshold, or a human development decision.

**The artifact evaluated on the holdout is the artifact that ships.** There is no retrain on the full snapshot after the holdout is opened: a refit model would be a different set of weights from the one the reported number describes. The consequence is a raw-model fit cutoff **152 delivery days before the snapshot cutoff** (1 + 60 + 1 + 90), disclosed rather than hidden.

**Four cutoffs are published separately** — on the release page and in the MLflow lineage tags — because they are four different dates: `snapshot_cutoff` (last delivery day in the committed snapshot), `raw_model_fit_cutoff` (last delivery day entering the raw quantile heads), `final_calibration_window` (start/end of the 60-day CQR slice), and `holdout_window` (start/end of the 90-day evaluation period).

**The one-shot report** contains champion and similar-day-naïve MAE, champion and naïve mean pinball loss, their percentage differences, final empirical coverage at 50/80/95, and the probabilistic daily-vector DM statistic, effect size and p-value. It carries this label, exactly, wherever it appears:

> Pre-specified one-shot holdout DM test on a fixed 90-day window — confirmatory-style, not power-qualified.

The window is fixed by partition design, not derived from a power calculation. It is **not a gate**: a result that does not support superiority forbids the superiority claim and nothing else. There is no re-tuning, revised analysis choice, or second holdout run after outcomes are opened.

**Required limitation, verbatim in the report:**

> The holdout is a single contiguous recent period, so it tests generalization to the most recent regime rather than repeated out-of-sample skill. Its partitions were pinned before development and it was evaluated once, after the complete model and calibration pipeline were frozen, with no subsequent tuning. The 90-day length was fixed by partition design, not by a power calculation, so the Diebold–Mariano result is confirmatory-style but not power-qualified. The model shown is exactly the model evaluated — no refit followed the holdout — so its raw-model fit cutoff precedes the snapshot cutoff by 152 delivery days. Sequential lag features may use earlier holdout observations exactly as they would in live forecasting; no holdout outcome entered fitting or a development decision. Enforcement is procedural: this is a solo build and the discipline is disclosed, not cryptographically guaranteed.

The probabilistic DM (b) is the one aligned with the project's thesis — it tests whether the *intervals* beat persistence, not just the median.

### 7.2 Post-gate information benchmark — one run, one number (v6.7)

A controlled ablation that **quantifies the value of post-gate information without ever shipping it.** It runs **once, at M2/CP-2**:

- **Two arms.** *Strict* = the selected champion catalog (no delivery-day A69). *A69-augmented* = the same catalog plus delivery-day A69 and its named derivatives (the A69-based residual load and `dunkelflaute_flag`) — and nothing else different.
- **Everything else identical:** same evaluation rows, same five pinned folds, same base features, same preprocessing, same seeds, same hyperparameter search space and tuning budget.
- **Run on raw quantile heads, before CQR and isotonic.** Neither arm is calibrated.
- **Exactly one headline:** pooled mean pinball loss across the nine quantiles and five folds, expressed as the **percentage difference** between arms. Median MAE may be included only if it comes free from the same stored predictions.
- **Do not report** coverage, interval width, or calibration error for an uncalibrated comparison.
- **It is a benchmark, never the champion.** The A69-augmented model is not calibrated, not registered, not deployed, and cannot be promoted; A69 is never reclassified KFT.

**Required limitation:**

> This uncalibrated raw-head comparison isolates the information content of post-gate A69. It makes no claim about calibrated interval quality and does not make A69 available at the forecast gate.

The benchmark is the audit-driven upgrade of the old as-archived posture: instead of quietly using post-gate data and disclosing it, the project ships the feasible model and *measures* what feasibility costs.

## 8. Diagnostics and Explainability

*(Structure carried verbatim from v5.5; features and strata relabeled.)* These diagnostics are the heart of the portfolio artifact.

### 8.1 SHAP on the median model

- Global SHAP summary plot for the `p50` head **of the champion** on the validation tail; top-10 ranking; two dependence plots drawn from the **selected** champion catalog (e.g. `residual_load_proxy` × hour-of-day if the augmented catalog was selected, otherwise the strongest selected features). The plots use only features actually in the selected catalog. The A69-based `dunkelflaute_flag` appears only in the §7.2 benchmark's SHAP, never the champion's.
- **Scoping (state explicitly):** SHAP on the p50 head explains the **central tendency**, not interval *width*. Interval width is driven by the inter-quantile spread and the CQR shift `Q`. If asked "what drives your uncertainty," the answer is the spread + `Q`, not the p50 SHAP. SHAP *explains* the selected champion and may *name* a headline among its features — it is **not** the incremental-value test (that is the §4.1 two-arm comparison). No ranking, overlap or direction is required; a low-importance result is a valid reportable outcome. Trebbien et al. (2023, *Energy & AI*) — SHAP on a gradient-boosted model of German day-ahead prices — is the published methodological twin to cite.

### 8.2 Permutation importance

Permutation importance on the validation tail via `sklearn.inspection.permutation_importance`. SHAP attributes predictions in expectation; permutation importance measures degradation under shuffling. Reporting both signals maturity and surfaces any SHAP-vs-permutation rank disagreement.

### 8.3 Regime-stratified error analysis

A table of MAE and mean pinball loss on subsets: all validation; **Dunkelflaute days** (**v6.3: a post-hoc evaluation stratum** — using A69-based scarcity to *stratify errors* is legitimate because it never feeds the champion); non-flag days; **crisis (2021-09-01 → 2022-12-31); pre-crisis; post-crisis (≥2023-01-01); negative-price hours** (the DE-specific stratum); weekday; weekend. **`n_obs` is reported prominently for every row, and thin subsets (notably Dunkelflaute days and crisis-fold rows) carry bootstrap confidence intervals** (the Month-1 bootstrap) and are read qualitatively. All three regimes are populated from eval folds (Section 5.1); finer stress rows (the Dec-2024 Dunkelflaute week, the Aug-2022 peak weeks) come from training-period backtesting. If MAE on Dunkelflaute days or coverage on negative-price hours is materially worse, the report says so and ties it to the exchangeability paragraph (Section 6.2).

### 8.4 Reliability diagram

Empirical vs. nominal coverage at 50% / 80% / 95% on the validation tail, plotted at **all three stages (v6.3): the raw LightGBM heads; the post-CQR / pre-isotonic output (the stage carrying the formal marginal guarantee, §6.2); and the final post-isotonic intervals (empirical)**. This quantifies **whether, where, and by how much** isotonic-last changes coverage; no direction is assumed before measurement. It also makes the §6.2 three-stage claim visible. **Bounded-target tail caveat (two-sided in this market):** the DA price is bounded at **+4,000** and **−500 EUR/MWh** (−600 from 2026-05-28). The upper bound is almost never approached at DE-LU hourly resolution — documented as negligible, exactly as in v5.5. The **lower bound is live**: the floor was touched in 2023, and on extreme-solar days the p2.5/p5 conformity residuals can be truncated, so the lowest intervals can under-cover conditionally near the floor. This is documented as a known tail caveat, surfaced by the negative-price stratum in the stratified table, and deliberately not engineered around (a floor-aware tail would reopen scope).

A small set of **eight mandatory property tests** asserts the invariants that must hold — deliberately **not** a regression matrix (§13). The first four carry forward from v6.3; items 5–8 are the v6.7 set:

3. **UTC/DST lag alignment.** On the spring-forward (23-local-hour) and fall-back (25-local-hour) days, assert that a 24h/168h lag in UTC retrieves the correct row and that no hour is dropped or duplicated. Guards the §4.0 integer-lag corruption.

## 11. Risk Register

### R-1 — Three-regime fragility
**Description.** Pre-crisis, crisis, and post-crisis price distributions are structurally distinct, with two opposing stress directions (high-price scarcity vs. negative-price abundance); training spans all three but the forecast serves the post-crisis regime. **Mitigations:** three-regime walk-forward CV with both stress folds (Section 5.1); `crisis_period` and `post_crisis` as first-class champion features (**v6.3:** `dunkelflaute_flag` is a §8.3 evaluation stratum / §7.2 benchmark feature, not a champion feature); per-regime and negative-price-stratum error analysis (Section 8.3); if coverage diverges on regime-shift folds (reliability diagram), the report names it as a regime-shift failure rather than glossing it. R-1 is the project's best interview narrative.

### R-2 — VRE-forecast vintage (the KFT trap) — resolved by strict-gate design
**Description.** The day-ahead wind/solar forecast's regulatory publication deadline (18:00 D-1) post-dates the 12:00 CET gate, and **the spike observed directly (2026-06-12) that initial TP publication itself is post-gate** (A69/A01 absent at a 15:35 CEST D-1 pull). The same spike observed A65/A01 present at 15:35 CEST D-1 — consistent with its own deadline, and the corroboration the §3 assumption rests on, but not a pre-gate capture. **Resolution: the risk is eliminated for the champion runtime path by construction** — delivery-day A69 is not KFT, so it and its derivatives are **excluded from the champion runtime schema** (§4, §5.2, §9.4); the champion uses gate-feasible runtime features only, and the value of the post-gate information is *measured* by the §7.2 benchmark rather than quietly consumed. **v6.7:** the champion's historical-VRE input is the D-2-bounded trailing window in §4.1 — a LAG feature with no fitted layer and therefore no fit lineage to police. **Residual concerns, both disclosed as assumptions rather than engineered around:** A65's pre-gate status (§3), and A75 revision (§5.2). Revision metadata is a confirmed dead end (constant `revisionNumber`; `createdDateTime` = pull wall-clock). R-2 shifts from *"a caveat we disclose"* to *"a runtime design boundary we enforce and test."*

### R-3 — Platform migration, API churn, and data quality
**Description.** Three concrete risks: (i) the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform was migrating through 2025–26 with community-reported endpoint timeouts, 403/429s, and response-format changes; (ii) known TP data-quality issues (missing TSO submissions, aggregation inconsistencies across Germany's four TSOs — 50Hertz, Amprion, TenneT DE, TransnetBW); (iii) token/access friction. **Spike observation (2026-06-12):** a clean session — no 403/429/migration symptoms; monitored, not closed. **Mitigations:** `entsoe-py` pinned (0.8.0) and upgraded deliberately; the **SFTP bulk path** as a second ingestion route (port-22 reachability untested from the spike sandbox — verify from the M3 only if needed; the chunked API is assessed sufficient for the M1 bulk size); **SMARD as cross-check and fallback-primary** (same Reg-543 data, CC BY, no auth) — the CP-1 reconciliation gate (ENTSO-E↔SMARD within €0.01/MWh on >99.9% of overlapping hours; **96/96 sample hours already within €0.01 at the spike**) converts the cross-check into a verified property.

### R-4 — 15-minute MTU transition
**Description.** The SDAC resolution change at delivery 2025-10-01 splits the target series into hourly-product and quarter-hourly-product eras; mishandled, it corrupts every lag and rolling feature after the boundary (the same failure class as DST, occurring once but permanently). **Spike findings (2026-06-12):** the boundary averaging path validated on samples (0 missing/0 duplicated hours; hourly = mean of 4 quarter-hours to 1e-9), **and** the feature feeds are PT15M across the whole archive — the aggregation discipline applies from day one, with the chunk-boundary partial-bin artifact as the live failure mode. **Mitigations:** the §0-item-4 averaging rule; §4.0's chunk-boundary bin-integrity requirement; `entsoe-py` 0.8.0 (date-switched resolution handling); the extended boundary invariant test (Section 9.4 item 4); Fold 5 deliberately evaluates entirely post-transition so the headline metric is measured on the series the product actually serves.

### R-5 — Spectral-EDA scope creep — added at v6.2, downgraded at v6.7
**Description.** The §4.2 frequency-domain work expands into filter design, wavelets/EMD, or frequency-domain forecasting, consuming capstone time for no hiring payoff. **Mitigations:** the block is capped at three committed figures and one paragraph, with a non-binding ~3 h estimate; wavelet/EMD/frequency-domain-forecasting work is out of scope by §4.2's guardrails. **v6.7:** the formal overrun-escalation protocol, the `<60s` runtime gate and the dependency-policing rule are retired — the scope boundary is the control, and a stated boundary that the owner can see is worth more than a procedure nobody runs. **Owner:** learner.

## 13. What This Project Is NOT

Each omission is a deliberate scope decision, defended and interview-quotable. The v6.0 conversion **re-ratified** every v5.5 boundary that survives the market swap and added two new ones; v6.1 closes the single gas reopen condition on spike evidence. The only entries that died with PJM are those whose subject matter died with it (gas-fallback machinery, the alert-sourcing risk).

**No multi-day-ahead forecast.** *"The target is the next delivery day, because that's what the 12:00 CET day-ahead auction clears and it's the horizon at which every lagged feature is legitimately known at forecast time. A multi-day forecast made at the gate would have to consume prices that haven't cleared yet — a look-ahead leak. I'd rather ship a clean 24-hour forecast than a leaky multi-day one."*

**"Why isn't the wind/solar forecast a feature?" (strict-gate design, v6.3)** — *"Because it isn't known when the model runs. The day-ahead auction closes at 12:00; I verified at the project's data spike that ENTSO-E's day-ahead wind and solar forecast for the delivery day isn't published until the 15:35–18:00 window — after the gate. Most published EPF work uses the as-archived series anyway and discloses it; I went further and excluded it from the shipped model entirely, so the champion is feasible at 12:00 by construction, using a gate-feasible residual-load proxy for the merit-order signal **where it earns its place in a comparison fixed before fitting** — and shipping without it if it doesn't. Then I quantified what I gave up: a controlled benchmark, identical except it adds the post-gate forecast, run on raw heads and reported as one number — the percentage difference in pooled pinball loss. So I ship an honest model and put a number on the value of information I chose not to use."*

**No quarter-hourly target (new, v6.0).** *"SDAC moved to 15-minute products in October 2025. I kept the hourly target end-to-end — averaging post-transition quarter-hours, which SDAC's own no-arbitrage constraint defines as the hourly equivalent — because a 7.5-year continuous hourly series with three regimes teaches the model more than nine months of quarter-hours, and because a normally 96-step target (92/100 on DST days) would have quadrupled the output dimension mid-project for no methodological gain. Quarter-hourly is the natural v2, and the boundary invariant test means the pipeline already handles the dual-resolution data correctly."*

**No standalone fuel-price layer (adjudicated closed, v6.1).** *"There is no free, daily, legally-redistributable TTF or THE gas series — Yahoo's exists but its terms prohibit redistribution, and I wasn't going to build a reproducible open repo on data I can't commit. I even probed SMARD's API at the project's de-risking spike: it exposes gas-fired generation volumes, not THE prices, so the pre-committed reopen condition closed on evidence. Residual load is the literature-standard German merit-order driver, so I tested a gate-feasible residual arm and kept it only if it lowered pooled pinball loss on the development folds; if it did not, the base champion and the A69 benchmark state that limitation explicitly."*

**"You had a second scarcity feature — what happened to it?" (scoped out, v6.7)** — *"`expected_scarcity_proxy` was `relu(load_z)·relu(−vre_exp_z)`: it fires only when load is above its mean **and** expected renewables are below theirs. The `relu`s and the product are the point — a ratio cannot encode an AND condition, a product can. I scoped it out when I cut the four-catalog experiment down to one comparison, because the machinery needed to adjudicate two correlated arms cost more than the feature was likely worth. It is a design I can defend, not a result I am claiming."*

**No formal causal claim.** *"The model makes a predictive claim with calibrated uncertainty, not a causal one. The perturbation control is a ceteris-paribus sensitivity probe — it shows how the model responds to a feature holding others fixed, which can be physically out-of-distribution, and the page says so. SHAP attributes predictions, it doesn't establish causation. I deliberately stayed on the predictive side of that line."*

**No enterprise production pipeline or live trading layer.** *"Containerized marimo on Hugging Face Spaces alongside a public DagsHub MLflow record is portfolio-grade hosting, not production infrastructure — no CI/CD matrices, no scheduled retraining, no live per-visitor API ingestion, no trading gates, no scheduled/production drift monitoring. **(v6.7: the v6.3 offline data/output-health report is deleted. It could not measure accuracy drift without labels, and it existed to imitate the operations surface this boundary says the project does not build. Ordinary input validation and schema tests remain.)** I invested in interactive risk communication and transparent experiment tracking, which showcase DS judgment, rather than enterprise MLOps boilerplate. DVC was rejected as collaboration overhead the project doesn't need; live per-visitor pulls as brittleness that would break the demo."*

**No neural challenger — no NBEATSx, TFT, or transformers.** *"The peer-reviewed EPF evidence shows classical and tree-based baselines within a few percent of deep learning on hub- and zone-level day-ahead price; the marginal accuracy doesn't earn the complexity for a portfolio. LightGBM is the published-evidence-backed default — and the recent German-market XAI literature (Trebbien et al.) runs on exactly this model class. I'd rather demonstrate judgment than run a model horse-race. I can build and train networks — there's a shipped CNN in my portfolio — so this is a choice, not a limitation."*

**No LEAR / `epftoolbox` baseline.** *"LEAR is the canonical EPF baseline but a specialized library that obscures the DS skills the project demonstrates. Ridge on the same engineered catalog plus the similar-day naïve and the 168h naïve covers the same transparent-classical-baseline role — and the similar-day naïve follows Lago's exact convention, so the comparison stays anchored to the published benchmark."*

**No sequential conformal (EnbPI / SPCI) and no Giacomini-White.** *"CQR gives finite-sample marginal coverage under exchangeability — the property an interview reader is most likely to recognize and probe. EnbPI/SPCI handle non-exchangeability more aggressively but add conceptual surface that distracts from the core argument. The reliability diagram makes the exchangeability violation visible, and the limitations section names it."* **v6.7 retires the automatic reopen condition.** Any regime-level coverage miss — including one larger than 10 percentage points — is reported and discussed; it does not add EnbPI, reopen the model, or block a checkpoint. Reintroducing sequential conformal work would require a new owner-ratified amendment.

**"Does isotonic break your CQR coverage guarantee?" (three-stage coverage, v6.3)** — *"It can, so I'm precise about it. CQR's finite-sample marginal guarantee attaches to the conformalized output, before the isotonic step. Isotonic is a monotone rearrangement I apply last only to kill residual quantile crossings; it does nothing **only when all post-CQR quantiles are already ordered**. The sign of a pair's conformal threshold is not enough to establish global ordering. Because isotonic can move interval edges whenever it acts, I report the final post-isotonic coverage as empirical and show all three stages — raw, post-CQR, final — on the reliability diagram, and I claim the formal guarantee only for the stage that actually has it."*

**No trading layer, battery arbitrage, or bid logic.** *"The deliverable is a forecast and a diagnostic report. Wrapping a forecast in a trading layer either needs real risk infrastructure or it's a toy — and a toy trading layer would undermine credibility. The forecast and its honestly-communicated uncertainty are the artifact."*

**No test matrix, no regression CI — but targeted invariant tests on a thin CI.** **Planned post-CP-1 interview answer, not a current-state claim:** use the following only after CP-1 has actually committed the eight tests and thin workflow required by §9.4. *"I wrote eight focused property tests on the invariants that silently corrupt results if violated: post-CQR/isotonic monotonicity; closed-left rolling windows; UTC/DST lag alignment; half-open PT15M delivery-day slicing plus complete hourly bins; the champion/benchmark runtime-schema firewall; the residual proxy's D-2, 42-day and DST boundaries; spectral peak labels; and tail-partition integrity. A separate hand-checkable CQR rank fixture verifies the order-statistic indexing and negative-threshold case. The schema test rejects delivery-day A69 and same-day actuals while admitting lagged A75 through D-2. There is no fitted VRE climatology or fit-lineage test in v6.7. A thin GitHub Actions workflow runs the properties on every push — the right level for a single-developer portfolio, without scheduled retraining, drift gates or rollback machinery."*

**"How did you choose your seasonal features?"** — *"I didn't just assume 24h and weekly cycles — I confirmed them. A Welch periodogram of the detrended price series shows clear peaks at 24h, 168h and a 12h harmonic, which is exactly what the EPF literature reports (Weron 2014; Lago et al. 2018). That's the empirical justification for my hour-of-day and day-of-week features. It only motivates testing a seasonal residual-load candidate; the M2 ablation — not the price spectrum — decides whether that candidate belongs in the champion."*

**"How do you know your walk-forward folds respect the regime changes?"** — *"Change-point detection with `ruptures` defines the pre-crisis, crisis, and normalization boundaries; the folds are not mechanically cut at those dates. I deliberately pin five 90-day evaluation blocks so they sample every regime and both stress directions, while each training window still expands through time with a one-delivery-day embargo. Per-regime periodograms show why I should not collapse those results into one recent-tail story: seasonal amplitude and the noise floor differ by regime. That is an evaluation-coverage argument, not a claim that spectral analysis prevents leakage."*

**"You have a signal-processing background — where did it help here?"** — *"The spectral EDA. I used Welch's method rather than a raw periodogram because the series is long, noisy and non-stationary; averaging modified periodograms with a Hann window trades a little frequency resolution for much lower variance, which is what you want when you're confirming known cycles rather than hunting faint tones."*

**"Why is your portfolio link a static page and not the live demo?"** — *"Because a recruiter's first impression shouldn't depend on whether a free-tier container happens to be awake. The primary URL is a static export of the full report — CDN-served, unable to sleep, and making zero runtime calls — while the interactive demo is one labeled click deeper, with the possible ~30-second wake stated up front. I do not promise a network-dependent load-time threshold. The small interaction is served by direct bundled-model inference or a one-dimensional lookup, whichever the implementation uses; nothing methodological lives only in the live app. Decoupling the first impression from the runtime is the design decision."*
