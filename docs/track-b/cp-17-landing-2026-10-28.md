## Open for the Owner and r18

From the freeze record §11, each written without any 4.7T outcome:

- **G1: shadow issuances.** The fallbacks' interval buffers hold each policy's own released errors.
  They go stale unless v4, v2 and B0 issue daily as shadow issuances, which are never published. This
  costs about 100 s a day on the issuing host.
- **C1: the label wording.** The brief said labels come from days ≤ D−2. The landed training
  targets run to D−1, whose prices are published on D−2. The code follows the landed rule, which
  CP-26's parity requires. The proposal: "labels" are the released errors (≤ D−2), and training
  targets are the days published strictly before the issue time.
- **C2: partial days.** The brief required every delivery hour of D. The landed design issues only
  the hours its inputs can forecast.
  - 43 of CP-26's 2,078 scored days are partial, mostly the days after a DST change.
  - Under the brief as written, they are failed issuances. The frozen code follows the brief until
    r18 rules.
  - The proposal: those hours count as successful, displayed as partial.
- **G2:** GFS missing in a training window, but not at the issue time, is an A1 failure, and v2
  issues. The proposal is to record it.
- **F1: one issuing-host parity miss, reported and not tuned.**
  - At the DST origin 2025-10-26, A1 under the issuing host differs from native by up to
    0.028 EUR/MWh, with every selection identical.
  - One configuration trained to the 1,000-epoch cap amplified the rounding.
  - τ_eq stays the visitor's tolerance against the issued artifact, which is bit for bit in both
    browsers.
- **The second review's non-blocking notes, N1–N7.** Among them:
  - the issuance orchestrator is not in the frozen manifest, and has no timeout;
  - an unmeasured attempt is not refused;
  - `freeze verify` does not recheck the Pyodide, Node.js and search-state hashes;
