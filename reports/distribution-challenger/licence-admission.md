## 2. Provenance of the DDNN implementation

**The code is original.** DDNN is written from scratch in this repository and licensed under the
repository's MIT licence ([`LICENSE`](../../LICENSE)). That covers the network, the Johnson SU head
and its likelihood, the analytic gradients, the optimizer, regularisation, the training loop, early
stopping, seeding, ensembling and quantile emission. Its home is `src/cp23/ddnn.py`, which imports
NumPy and the Python standard library only (§18.2, §21.3; the import audit is in `tests/cp23/`).

**Nothing third-party was copied.** No third-party DDNN code was copied or adapted, and no
pretrained weights are used: every network starts from a seeded random initialisation and is
trained at its own origin. The implementation follows the published mathematics in the sources
below, not any code.

**Method sources:**

| Element | Source |
|---|---|
| Distributional neural network with a Johnson SU head for day-ahead electricity prices | G. Marcjasz, M. Narajewski, R. Weron, F. Ziel (2023). *Distributional neural networks for electricity price forecasting.* Energy Economics 125, 106843 |
| The Johnson SU family: density, quantile function, parameterisation | N. L. Johnson (1949). *Systems of frequency curves generated by methods of translation.* Biometrika 36(1/2), 149–176 |
| Optimizer | D. P. Kingma, J. Ba (2015). *Adam: A method for stochastic optimization.* ICLR |
| Weight initialisation | X. Glorot, Y. Bengio (2010). *Understanding the difficulty of training deep feedforward neural networks.* AISTATS |

**Inputs.** DDNN's design matrix comes from the project's existing admitted feature pipeline:
CP-15's 23-feature normalised design, CP-20's three frozen GFS columns and their missing-value
indicators (§21.2). That pipeline is outside the NumPy-only rule (§18.2 item 3).
